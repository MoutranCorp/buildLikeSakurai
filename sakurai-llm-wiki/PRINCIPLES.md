# Sakurai Principles

## Feel And Timing
- Use timing as a material. Pauses, hit stop, acceleration, recoil, and release are not garnish; they shape impact.
- Count frames when feel matters. Vague timing language produces vague play.
- Avoid sluggishness unless the sensation of heaviness is itself the point.
- Fast retries preserve motivation better than long explanations after failure.
- Screen shake, slow motion, trails, and flash only matter when they strengthen the player's read of the moment.
- Weight comes from a stack of cues: startup, motion arc, hit reaction, sound, camera, and recovery.
- Competitive depth does not require bloated execution complexity.

## Clarity And Readability
- If the player cannot read the result quickly, the design is weaker than it looks on paper.
- Important elements should be bigger, louder, brighter, or otherwise easier to parse than secondary ones.
- Style is valuable, but not when it conceals function.
- Text should be legible at real play distance, not only in a mockup.
- Menus, HUDs, and loading screens define the player's impression of the world just as much as levels do.
- Make important actions and states readable even without words when possible.
- Color, contrast, scale, and motion should guide attention intentionally.

## Rewards And Motivation
- Show the player what they are aiming at.
- Praise the player clearly when they do something well.
- Plan rewards early; they are structure, not decoration.
- Good randomness spices play up without erasing ownership or learning.
- If restarting feels bad, the reward loop is probably too weak or too delayed.
- Let players feel clever, including through light "cheating" or bending the system in satisfying ways.
- A game can make even work-like actions enjoyable if the framing and payoff are right.

## Teaching And Accessibility
- Teach through play whenever possible.
- Start from what players already know and build upward.
- Broad input with broad output makes games easier to approach without making them shallow.
- Beginners need kindness, but not condescension.
- Tutorials should feel natural, short, and context-aware.
- Help text belongs where confusion actually happens.
- Difficulty support and handicap systems can preserve social play and long-term engagement.

## Concept And Essence
- Protect the concept. Do not dilute the core promise with unrelated flourishes.
- More "essence" is not always better if it narrows appeal too sharply.
- Ask what the game is really about before optimizing its details.
- A mechanic belongs when it reinforces the fantasy, not merely when it is clever.
- Sequels should justify themselves with a fresh purpose, not habit.
- Licensed works should honor the original piece before showing off reinterpretation.
- A project can succeed with surprisingly few inputs if the output space is rich enough.

## Presentation Layers
- Graphics affect fun when they clarify play, sharpen mood, or sell scale and identity.
- Animation should communicate force, intent, and vulnerability, not only motion.
- Audio is not secondary; weak sound drags the whole game down.
- Reverb, ambience, music tempo, and attack sounds all change perceived weight and world feel.
- Exaggeration often compensates for the information lost in translation from reality to screen.
- Effects should fit the size, speed, and fiction of the event they represent.
- Visual style should unify the game rather than fragment it.

## Production And Direction
- A director's job includes cutting, resolving, and aligning, not just inventing.
- Keep proposals fast, persuasive, and concrete.
- Organize files, parameters, and tasks so iteration stays cheap.
- Communicate early; silence creates rework.
- Internal pressure should stay high even when deadlines seem distant.
- Teams lose output when too many people create coordination overhead.
- Play testing, bug testing, and tuning are not end phases; they continue deep into development.

## Marketing And External Framing
- Paint an accurate picture of the game.
- Show the real game as soon as you can.
- Marketing multiplies what is already there; it does not rescue a weak core.
- Avoid comparisons that trap the audience into judging the wrong thing.
- The first promise matters because it becomes the lens through which everything else is read.
- Website copy, reveal videos, demos, and store messaging are part of design communication.

## Time Respect
- Be mindful of the time players are giving you.
- Skip options, pause options, and quick transitions are acts of respect.
- Good errands move the player toward something meaningful; bad errands stall momentum.
- Remove needless waits in menus, retries, travel, and setup.
- If a feature asks for time, it should repay that time with clarity, delight, mastery, or surprise.
